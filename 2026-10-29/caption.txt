Your PL/pgSQL function threw "invalid transaction termination"? You needed a procedure.

A plpgsql function returns a value. It lives inside your query, and you call it with SELECT.

A PostgreSQL procedure has no RETURNS clause. You run it on its own with CALL.

Here's what actually matters: only a procedure can COMMIT or ROLLBACK while it runs. So batch jobs belong in procedures. On a 10,000-row test table, archive_orders moved 4,126 old orders into orders_archive, 1,000 rows per batch, with a COMMIT after each one.

Pick the wrong one and you'll know fast. Slides 6 to 8 show the 3 real errors you get.

Put COMMIT in a function and you get invalid transaction termination.

SELECT on a procedure, or CALL on a function, fails with a HINT that names the right one.

Run CALL inside BEGIN and it fails too, the moment your procedure tries to commit.

Procedures need PostgreSQL 11 or later. Tested on PostgreSQL 16.

Save this for the next batch job you write.
