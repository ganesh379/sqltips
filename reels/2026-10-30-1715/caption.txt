In PostgreSQL, an UPDATE does not overwrite your row. It writes a new version.

We updated Ravi's salary from 50000 to 60000. The ctid moved from (0,1) to (0,2): same page, new slot. The old version stayed in slot 1.

That is MVCC in PostgreSQL. The old row version is kept while another transaction may still need to see it. Once nobody needs it, it is a dead row, and pg_stat_user_tables showed 1 live and 1 dead.

VACUUM removed the dead row and marked the space for reuse. Lots of updates without vacuum is how table bloat starts.
