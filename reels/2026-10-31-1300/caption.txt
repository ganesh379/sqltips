One NULL can make your NOT IN query return 0 rows.

NOT IN checks your value against every value in the list. Any comparison with NULL gives UNKNOWN, and WHERE drops every row that is not TRUE.

The fix: use NOT EXISTS, or filter the NULLs out of the subquery with WHERE customer_id IS NOT NULL.

Same behaviour in PostgreSQL and Oracle.
